01 · Profile

Between Text and Lived Experience

I am interested in how rules are formed, interpreted, and experienced in everyday life. Coursework has given me a foundation in doctrinal analysis and comparative research. Fieldwork has taught me to understand a setting before reaching a conclusion, while sustained student responsibilities have taught me to listen, coordinate, and respond. Research and practice are not separate paths for me; together, they help me understand law more carefully and people more concretely.

02 · Selected Work

Research and Practice

My interests extend from international law and environmental human rights to corporate responsibility, grassroots legal governance, and social research. Across these projects, I bring doctrinal analysis, comparative research, and field-based observation into the same line of inquiry.

01

Individual Academic Paper · 2025

Environmental Human Rights in the Context of Climate Change

Taking the International Court of Justice’s advisory opinion on climate change as a starting point, I examined the legal status, core content, and state obligations associated with the right to a clean, healthy, and sustainable environment. The paper considers how judicial caution may support minimum consensus while also limiting the justiciability of the right.

International law and human-rights analysis · Doctrinal reasoning · Literature review

02

Student Innovation Project · Team Member

A Chinese Path toward Criminalizing Corporate Supervisory Duties

As a member of a student research team, I contributed to a comparative study of corporate compliance and criminal liability in the United States, the United Kingdom, France, and Italy. The project examined organizational liability, supervisory negligence, and possible institutional approaches in the Chinese context.

Criminal law and corporate compliance · Comparative legal research · Team research

03

Field Research · 2024

Grassroots Governance and the Rule of Law

I joined a six-member team studying grassroots governance and the rule of law. For the Zhuhai Xiangzhou community case, I contacted local participants, prepared interview questions, conducted field observation, organized materials, and contributed to the final report. The research covered grid-based governance, ageing, fraud prevention, the digital divide, and community legal services.

Field observation · Interview preparation · Community communication · Report writing

04

Summer Field Project · 2025

University Student Travel Consumption

Working with a nine-member team, I helped study how emotional value, budgeting, peers, and social media shape university students’ travel decisions. The team collected 57 valid questionnaires, and I contributed to the related analysis and report writing.

Survey research · Consumer-behavior observation · Collaborative writing

For me, entering the field is not a single research trip or volunteer shift, but a line of action sustained over years: asking a question, explaining an idea to peers, sharing a child’s perspective with educators, and later returning to life education as a university volunteer.
